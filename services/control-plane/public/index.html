How it works

Stage 10 is the part nobody else runs

Every other reviewer stops at an opinion. Cavix keeps going. It writes a failing test that reproduces the bug, applies its own fix, and runs your whole suite again. Three exit codes decide whether you ever hear about it.

1. Reproduce it

Cavix writes a test that makes the bug happen on purpose, then runs it in a sealed sandbox. A failing exit code is the only acceptable answer here.

2. Fix it

It applies the fix it is about to suggest and runs that same test again. If the test does not turn green, the suggestion was wrong and gets thrown away.

3. Check nothing broke

Your full suite runs one more time. A fix that solves one bug and starts another is not a fix, and Cavix would rather stay quiet than hand you that trade.

Proven, or silent.

A reviewer earns its place by being right, not by being loud. The moment a tool posts something it cannot stand behind, developers learn to scroll past it, and every finding after that is wasted, including the correct ones.

So the rule is blunt: a finding that cannot be reproduced is suppressed. Not softened, not tagged low confidence. Suppressed. What lands on your pull request is the set of problems that were made to happen on purpose, then made to stop.
